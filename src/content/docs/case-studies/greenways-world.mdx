The Greenways.ai world is the public-facing spatial language for **connected tools, standards, habitats, and people who build**. It is not a single palace, a mascot garden, or a highbrow fantasy narrative. The job of the world is to make the viewer feel that they can enter, understand the routes, and make something of their own.

## Day is orientation; night is discovery

Daylight should help anyone read the world quickly. Routes connect visible places. Tools and standards belong in civic spaces rather than appearing as abstract technology claims.

Night does not hide the world. It reveals another layer: observatories in use, harbour routes, conservatories glowing inside a cold landscape, quiet libraries in forests, reflected settlements, and wildlife behaving naturally.

## How it supports greenways.ai

The public site can use this world as a carousel of real places rather than a sequence of slogans. Each place carries a clear role:

- **World Confluence** introduces the connected-world idea.
- **Jeweled Archipelago** shows communities linked without becoming identical.
- **Solar Steppe Observatories** shows accessible instruments and open standards.
- **Archive Forest Basin** places knowledge inside living habitat rather than above it.
- **Aurora Conservatory Belt** shows infrastructure as something people inhabit and maintain.

The copy around the imagery stays plain: anyone can build, places connect, tools are usable, standards let worlds interoperate, and Greenways contributes heavily to open source without turning the entire public site into an OSS pitch.

## What to avoid

- one giant temple or throne as the centre of the brand;
- invented chapter headings and ceremonial language that distance the viewer;
- yellow luxury copy sitting on top of peacock imagery;
- a single animal treated as the only subject at world scale;
- cyberpunk cities, robots, hologram people, or generic AI spectacle;
- unrelated day and night images presented as one place.
